06Ultrasonic Sensor, Touch Sensor and PIR Sensor

Non-contact sensing of distance with sound waves, sensing of human touch, and detection of the presence of a warm moving body using infrared radiation — the three sensors used most often in student projects and in building automation.

  2. Touch Sensor

1.Ultrasonic Sensor

Definition

An ultrasonic sensor measures distance or detects the presence of an object by transmitting a sound wave of frequency above the human hearing range (above 20 kHz, usually 40 kHz) and measuring the time taken for the echo to return from the object.

Principle of sound and its speed

Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave and therefore needs a medium. Its speed depends on the medium and on temperature. In dry air at 0 °C the speed is 331 m/s and it increases by about 0.6 m/s for each degree Celsius rise. At 20 °C the speed is about 343 m/s. In water it is about 1480 m/s and in steel about 5900 m/s. The relationship between speed v, frequency f and wavelength λ is v = fλ. For 40 kHz in air, λ = 343/40000 ≈ 8.6 mm, which is why ultrasonic sensors can detect quite small objects.

Formula

Distance from time of flightd = (v × t) / 2where d is the distance to the object in metres, v is the speed of sound in m/s and t is the total round-trip time in seconds. The factor 2 appears because the wave travels to the object and back.
With v = 343 m/s and t in microseconds: d (cm) = t (µs) / 58.

Construction and transducer types

  • Piezoelectric transducer — the most common. A piezoelectric ceramic disc (lead zirconate titanate, PZT) is bonded to a metal diaphragm. Applying an alternating voltage makes the disc vibrate and produce sound (transmitter mode); receiving sound makes the disc vibrate and produce a voltage (receiver mode). The same element can work in both modes.
  • Electrostatic / capacitive transducer — a thin membrane and a fixed back plate; used in high-quality microphones and in some wide-bandwidth ultrasonic sensors.
  • Electromagnetic / magnetostrictive — used at lower frequencies and higher powers.
  • Open versus sealed — open transducers are used in air; sealed ones are used underwater or in harsh environments.

The HC-SR04 module

The HC-SR04 is the ultrasonic module most used in teaching laboratories. It contains a transmitter, a receiver, an oscillator, a driver and a control circuit, all on a small printed board with four pins.

Working sequence

  1. The microcontroller sends a HIGH pulse of at least 10 µs to the Trig pin.
  2. The module internally generates a burst of eight cycles of 40 kHz and transmits it through the transmitter, and at the same time drives the Echo pin HIGH.
  3. The sound wave travels to the object and is reflected back. The receiver detects the echo, amplifies and filters it, and when the echo is recognised the module drives the Echo pin LOW.
  4. The width of the HIGH pulse on the Echo pin is therefore exactly the time of flight t.
  5. The microcontroller measures this width using a timer, an input capture unit, or a function such as pulseIn() in Arduino.
  6. The distance is calculated as d = t × 343 / 2 metres, or d (cm) = t (µs) / 58.
Example

Numerical 1. The echo pulse width measured is 1166 µs. Distance d = 1166/58 = 20.1 cm.
Numerical 2. An object is at 1.5 m. Round-trip distance = 3.0 m, so t = 3.0/343 = 8.75 ms = 8746 µs of echo pulse width.

Modes of operation

  • Echo / pulse-echo mode — one transducer both transmits and receives. Used for distance measurement and level measurement.
  • Through-beam / transmitted mode — separate transmitter and receiver facing each other; an object interrupting the beam is detected. Used for object counting and intrusion detection.
  • Reflective / proximity mode — a simple threshold is used to say only whether an object is present within a set range.
  • Doppler mode — the frequency shift of the echo from a moving object gives its velocity. Used in traffic speed guns and in medical blood-flow measurement.

Factors affecting accuracy, advantages and limitations

The reading is affected by temperature (the speed of sound changes by 0.17 % per °C, so a temperature compensation using an LM35 is often added), by humidity and air flow, by the angle and texture of the target (a soft or angled surface scatters the sound away and gives a weak or missing echo), by beam width (objects outside the 15° cone are not seen, and a narrow object may give a false long reading), and by multiple reflections in enclosed spaces. The minimum distance of about 2 cm exists because the receiver is still ringing from the transmitted burst.

  • Advantages: non-contact; unaffected by colour, transparency or dust; works in darkness; measures almost any solid or liquid surface; cheap and simple to interface; not affected by electromagnetic interference.
  • Limitations: needs a medium, so it cannot work in vacuum; short range; affected by temperature and by soft, absorbent or highly angled surfaces; limited angular resolution; relatively slow update rate.

Applications

  • Distance measurement and obstacle avoidance in mobile robots, drones and automated guided vehicles.
  • Parking assistance and reversing sensors in automobiles.
  • Liquid level measurement in tanks, bore wells, overhead tanks and in the chemical industry (non-contact, so suitable for corrosive liquids).
  • Level measurement of solids such as grain, cement and coal in silos.
  • Flow measurement in open channels using the level-area method.
  • Thickness measurement of plates and pipes, and flaw detection (ultrasonic testing).
  • Medical imaging (ultrasonography) and measuring the thickness of tissue.
  • Burglar alarms, automatic door openers, people counting, and robotic vacuum cleaners.

2.Touch Sensor

Definition

A touch sensor is a device that detects the presence or the making of physical contact, usually by a human finger or by an object, and converts it into an electrical signal. It gives essentially a binary output: touched or not touched.

Types of touch sensor

  1. Mechanical (contact) type — a push button, a microswitch, a limit switch or a whisker. Contact physically closes a pair of contacts. Simple, cheap and reliable, but the moving parts wear out after some millions of operations.
  2. Resistive type — two conductive layers separated by small spacers. Pressing makes them touch and the resistance between them falls. Used in resistive touchscreens and in force sensitive pads.
  3. Capacitive type — the most common modern type. A conductive pad forms one plate of a capacitor and the human finger, which is conductive, forms the other. Touching increases the capacitance, and an electronic circuit detects this change. There are no moving parts, so the pad can be placed behind glass or plastic and the surface can be completely sealed. Used in mobile phone screens, touch lamps and appliance panels.
  4. Piezoelectric type — a piezo element generates a charge when tapped; used for detecting knock or impact.
  5. Optical / infrared touch — a grid of IR beams across the front of a screen; a finger interrupts two beams and the position is calculated. Used in large interactive displays and kiosks.
  6. Surface acoustic wave — ultrasonic waves travel across a glass surface and are absorbed by a touch.
  7. Strain gauge / load cell type — detects the small deflection of a panel under pressure.

Working of a capacitive touch sensor

A capacitive touch circuit measures capacitance in one of several ways: by charging the pad and counting the time to reach a threshold, by measuring the change in the frequency of an oscillator to which the pad is connected, or by transferring charge between the pad and a reference capacitor in a switched-capacitor arrangement. In all cases the finger adds a capacitance of roughly 5 pF to 30 pF to a pad that itself may be 10 pF to 50 pF, so the total may rise by 20 % to 50 %, which is easily detected.

Dedicated ICs make this simple. The TTP223 is a single-channel capacitive touch IC in a small SOT-23 package that works from 2 V to 5.5 V, draws only about 3 µA, and gives a push-button style digital output; the output can be configured as latching or momentary and as active high or active low by two soldering options. The MPR121 handles 12 channels over an I2C bus and the CAP1188 handles 8 channels. Microcontrollers such as the ATtiny and many ARM devices also have built-in capacitive touch peripherals.

Interfacing a simple mechanical touch switch

A mechanical switch connected to a microcontroller input pin must have a pull-up or pull-down resistor, otherwise the pin floats when the switch is open and the reading is unpredictable. With an internal pull-up enabled, the pin reads HIGH when the switch is open and LOW when it is closed to ground. In addition, the contacts bounce for a few milliseconds when they close, producing many rapid transitions. Bouncing is removed by a hardware RC filter (for example 10 kΩ and 100 nF, giving a time constant of 1 ms) or, more commonly, by software debouncing: read the pin, wait 20 ms, read again, and accept the value only if both readings agree.

Applications

  • Keypads and control panels of washing machines, microwave ovens, induction cookers, air conditioners and lifts.
  • Touch screens of mobile phones, tablets, ATMs, ticket machines and information kiosks.
  • Touch lamps and decorative lighting.
  • Robot skin and safety bumpers; whisker sensors on small mobile robots.
  • Limit switches and end stops in machine tools and 3D printers.
  • Emergency stop buttons, door interlocks and machine guards.
  • Object presence detection in packaging and assembly machines.
  • Medical equipment panels that must be sealed and easy to disinfect.

3.PIR Sensor (Passive Infrared Sensor)

Definition

A PIR sensor detects the infrared radiation emitted by warm objects, particularly the human body, and produces an electrical output when a warm body moves across its field of view. It is called passive because it does not emit any radiation of its own — it only receives, unlike an ultrasonic or an IR sensor which actively transmits.

Every object above absolute zero emits infrared radiation. The human body, at about 37 °C, radiates most strongly at a wavelength of about 9.4 µm, in the far infrared region. This radiation is what the PIR sensor detects.

Construction

  • Pyroelectric element — a crystal or ceramic such as lithium tantalate (LiTaO3), gallium nitride, or triglycine sulfate (TGS), which develops an electric charge on its surface when its temperature changes. This is the pyroelectric effect. Two such elements are used, connected in series opposition (back to back).
  • Fresnel lens — a white dome of moulded polyethylene that focuses the infrared onto the elements and, more importantly, divides the field of view into a number of alternating zones. The lens also blocks visible light, since polyethylene is opaque to visible light but transparent to far infrared.
  • Filter window — the sensor package has a window that passes only the 7 µm to 14 µm band, rejecting most other wavelengths.
  • JFET buffer and signal processing — inside the metal can, a JFET converts the high-impedance pyroelectric signal into a usable form. On the module, an IC such as the BISS0001 provides amplification, filtering, window comparison, delay timing and output drive.

Working principle

A pyroelectric material produces a charge only when its temperature is changing. If a person stands perfectly still in front of the sensor, its temperature becomes constant and the output falls to zero. That is why PIR sensors detect motion rather than mere presence.

The two pyroelectric elements are connected in opposition. When the environment is stable, both receive the same radiation and their outputs cancel. When a warm body moves across the field of view, the Fresnel lens causes it to pass from one zone to the next, so it first illuminates one element and then the other. This produces a differential signal: a positive pulse followed by a negative pulse (or the reverse, depending on the direction of motion). Using two elements in this way greatly reduces false triggering caused by a uniform change in room temperature, by sunlight moving across the room, or by electromagnetic interference.

This small alternating signal is amplified (gain of several thousand), passed through a band-pass filter of about 0.1 Hz to 10 Hz (which corresponds to the speed of a walking human and rejects both slow drift and fast electrical noise), and compared with a threshold in a window comparator. When the signal exceeds the threshold, an output pulse is generated whose duration is set by an external timing network.

The module has three pins: VCC, OUT and GND, and two blue preset potentiometers — one to set the sensitivity (detection distance) and one to set the delay time. A jumper selects the repeatable or non-repeatable trigger mode. The output can directly drive a transistor or a small relay module, or be read by a microcontroller input.

Advantages, limitations and installation notes

  • Advantages: completely passive, so it emits nothing and consumes almost no power; detects a person through the lens without any contact; wide angle and good range; very cheap; gives a clean digital output; works in total darkness.
  • Limitations: detects only movement of a warm body, not a stationary person; cannot see through glass or walls; range falls in hot weather because the difference between body temperature and room temperature is smaller; may be falsely triggered by pets, by hot air currents from an air conditioner or a heater, by curtains moving in sunlight, or by radio interference if the wiring is poor.
  • Installation: mount at a height of 2 m to 2.5 m, aim across the likely path of a person rather than straight at a door, keep away from heat sources, air vents, direct sunlight and fluorescent lamps, and allow 30 s to 60 s for the sensor to stabilise after switching on (it produces false triggers during warm-up).

Applications

  • Automatic lighting control in corridors, staircases, washrooms, parking areas and godowns — the most common use.
  • Burglar and intruder alarms; security systems combined with cameras and GSM alerting.
  • Automatic door openers and automatic flush systems.
  • Occupancy sensing in air conditioning and building management systems to save energy.
  • Counting people entering a room or a shop.
  • Wildlife cameras and animal detection.
  • Night-vision and thermal detection aids, and search-and-rescue equipment.
  • Toys, greeting cards and novelty items that respond to a person approaching.

★Summary

  • An ultrasonic sensor transmits a 40 kHz burst and measures the round-trip time of the echo; distance d = (v × t)/2, with v = 343 m/s in air at 20 °C.
  • In practical units, distance in cm = echo pulse width in µs divided by 58.
  • The HC-SR04 works from 5 V, measures 2 cm to 400 cm with about 3 mm accuracy, needs a 10 µs trigger pulse and returns a pulse width on the Echo pin.
  • Ultrasonic sensors are non-contact and unaffected by colour or dust, but need a medium, have a narrow beam, and are affected by temperature and by soft or angled surfaces.
  • A touch sensor gives a binary contact indication. Types include mechanical, resistive, capacitive, piezoelectric, optical and surface acoustic wave; capacitive types have no moving parts and work through glass.
  • Mechanical touch switches need a pull-up resistor and debouncing, in hardware with an RC network or in software by re-reading after about 20 ms.
  • A PIR sensor is passive and detects a change of infrared radiation. Two pyroelectric elements connected in opposition produce a differential signal only when a warm body moves across the zones of a Fresnel lens.
  • The PIR signal is amplified, filtered between 0.1 Hz and 10 Hz, and compared with a threshold; the HC-SR501 gives a 3.3 V output for an adjustable delay of 5 s to 200 s.
  • PIR sensors are used for automatic lighting and intruder alarms; they detect movement, not a stationary person, and cannot see through glass.
